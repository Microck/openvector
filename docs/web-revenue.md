# Funding OpenVector on the web

Start with one direct sponsor. OpenVector already gives a designer a reason to visit, and scanning a sponsor's short message takes no time away from the conversion. Keep individual tracing free. Offer a metered business service when teams ask to automate batches.

## Direct sponsorship

Sell one four-week placement to a design-tool, asset-library, or print-workflow company. Offer a short text line and one link on the opening screen. Label it **Advertisement** immediately beside the creative. Use a normal link. This keeps third-party scripts out of the workspace. The FTC gives “Ad” and “Advertisement” as examples of clear disclosures. [FTC native advertising guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/native-advertising-guide-businesses)

Collect a month of aggregate traffic first. A one-page media kit should give the dates, actual pageviews, audience evidence, placement mockup and what the sponsor will receive. Ask a relevant sponsor to try **$250 for four weeks** as a price experiment. At 25,000 delivered impressions that costs them $10 per thousand. At 5,000 it costs $50 per thousand. Agree on the price, reporting method and remedy for underdelivery before invoicing. A sponsor-specific destination URL lets the sponsor measure referrals without giving it access to the editor. A month with no booking earns $0.

## Network ads

EthicalAds reports around **$2.50 per 1,000 pageviews** for publishers assuming most traffic is from Europe and North America. Its network targets developers. Check whether it accepts OpenVector's actual mix of designers and developers. At that published rate, 10,000 eligible pageviews in a month imply about **$25**, 50,000 about **$125**, and 100,000 about **$250**. These scenarios assume comparable traffic and monetization. Repeated traces in one open tab do not create fresh pageviews. Measure realized revenue per thousand pageviews after launch. [EthicalAds publisher terms and calculator](https://www.ethicalads.io/publishers/) · [Google page RPM definition](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/112030)

For AdSense, an original tutorial on tracing a logo, including the source PNG and a discussion of the resulting paths, is a better candidate than an empty workspace. Google restricts ads on screens without publisher content or with low-value content and warns about accidental clicks near download buttons. Keep ads clear of the picker, preview and export controls. For personalized Google ads in the EEA, UK and Switzerland, budget for a Google-certified consent management platform. Treat approval, fill rate, geography and ad blocking as unknowns until measured. Place network ads on the landing page only. The editor is cross-origin isolated, which blocks third-party ad frames and scripts. Compare revenue and editor opens per 1,000 landing visits with and without the ad. [Publisher content rules](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/11112688) · [Placement policy](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/1346295) · [Consent requirements](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/13554116)

## Commercial volume

The repository's MIT license grants commercial use, redistribution and sublicensing, subject to its notice requirement. A business can self-host the released engine or run the CLI. OpenVector can charge for its own hosted processing, support and service commitments. A browser-only counter is easy to reset, and file contents and tracing currently stay on the user's device. [MIT license](https://opensource.org/license/mit)

Offer a separate opt-in batch endpoint for organizations. As an initial price test, allow **1,000 successful hosted conversions per organization per month**, then **$20 per additional 1,000**, prorated. Make the free cap a hard stop until an admin opts into billing. Ten paying organizations doing 5,000 conversions each would generate **$800/month gross**, before compute, storage, payment fees, support and tax. Count across the organization so multiple seats share the allowance. Volume measures usage, not company size. If the policy really depends on headcount or revenue, use a negotiated enterprise agreement with declared eligibility.

Issue organization API keys and reserve quota atomically before accepting a job. Release reservations for failed or canceled jobs. Give each job an idempotency key, record one billable success, and send that event to the billing provider with a stable identifier. Keep the local usage ledger authoritative for enforcing caps because billing event summaries can lag. Show the allowance and overage price before the admin enables paid use. A hosted job sends image bytes to the service, so disclose retention and deletion terms at that entry point. Measure cost per successful conversion by image size before offering uncapped batches. Vectorizer.AI already sells separate web and API access, a useful comparison for testing demand.
